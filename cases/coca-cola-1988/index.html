Period: Purchases during 1988–1989; retrospective analysis in Berkshire's 1989 and 1991 letters.

Verified historical position

Berkshire began investing in Coca-Cola during 1988 and accumulated more in 1989. The 1989 letter's common-stock table lists 23.35 million shares at a cumulative cost of approximately $1.024 billion. Buffett describes how he knew Coca-Cola as a consumer for decades yet had not previously converted that awareness into ownership.

His explanation highlights Roberto Goizueta's leadership from 1981, international business opportunities, global consumer acceptance and the combined force of marketing and capital allocation. These are explanatory judgments in the letter, not a guarantee of future growth.

Growth versus re-rating

Buffett's 1991 letter gives a useful counterweight to triumphalist retellings. Coca-Cola and Gillette appreciated rapidly as their earnings grew and market prices assigned a larger multiple to those earnings. Buffett specifically distinguishes the two mechanisms and cautions that a valuation re-rating cannot recur indefinitely.

This is an essential research discipline: the price of a share is not the same as the economic progress of the business. A strong enterprise may still be unattractive if its price already includes exceptionally favorable future assumptions.

Questions to carry forward

  • Can the business hold price and distribution through competitive changes?
  • What portion of reported growth is volume, price or new markets?
  • Does additional revenue require major asset investment?
  • Have share repurchases, dilution and dividends improved per-share outcomes?
  • How much of the historical investor gain came from cash-flow growth versus a different multiple?

Limitations: The $1.024 billion is a historical 1989 cost-basis figure. Do not treat it as today's Berkshire ownership or today's valuation of Coca-Cola.
