Period: November 2009 acquisition announcement. Primary evidence: joint Berkshire–BNSF transaction release filed with the SEC.

Documented terms

The boards of Berkshire and Burlington Northern Santa Fe announced an agreement under which Berkshire would acquire the remaining 77.4% of the railroad's equity it did not already own, with consideration worth $100 per share in a cash-and-stock package. The announced transaction valuation was about $44 billion, including approximately $10 billion of BNSF debt.

The company described the acquisition as tied to U.S. transportation infrastructure and the country's long-term economic development. This is a contemporaneous corporate explanation, not a precise private valuation forecast.

Why this is analytically different from See's

A railroad network is difficult to replicate in some corridors and has potentially long-lived commercial utility, but it must continually maintain tracks, equipment and other capital assets. It is therefore inappropriate to infer that Buffett's method required every attractive business to be asset-light.

A sensible valuation must normalize freight volumes through recession and recovery, labor and energy costs, competition, maintenance expenditure, asset-life assumptions, leverage and regulation. A strong competitive position can coexist with substantial reinvestment needs.

What to examine

  1. Network utilization and incremental economics.
  2. Capital required merely to maintain safety and existing service.
  3. Long-run pricing versus fuel, labor and competitive pressures.
  4. The quality and duration of contracts and customer relationships.
  5. Transaction price versus reasonable operating cash-flow scenarios.

Limits: The November announcement does not establish closing date or investment performance. Review later filings to understand integration and economic results.
